Well-Being Ring / Well-Being Design Series / Episode 07

The End.

A design is not finished until a stressed person can use it. Testing is the next step, and the one that matters most.

This last episode says where the project stands, what I would test next and with whom, what must be true before any real launch, and what the project taught me.

A concept, not yet a product.

(Status: concept and clickable HTML prototype. Not tested with real users. All data in the prototype is simulated.)

Everything in this series is a design argument, checked against the prototype and my competitor research. Nothing here is a result from users. The table shows what exists and what is still unknown.

02  Usability test plan

Test the decision, not the screen.

(Plan only. No sessions have run yet. Method: think-aloud sessions with 5 to 8 people who feel stressed regularly.)

The question is not whether the screens look right. It is whether a stressed person, with little energy, can follow one action to the end and say whether it helped. Each task is one step of that loop.

TaskWhat the person doesWhat I measure
Read HomeLooks at the orb and status line, then says what to do nextCan they name the next step in their own words, without help?
Main buttonTaps the main action for their stateDo they find it without searching? How long does it take?
BreathingFollows the session, or ends it earlyDo they finish? Did the pace feel right, in their words?
ResultReads the before and after card and the next stepDo they understand what changed? What do they press next?
Still tenseReads the gentle resultDo they choose “Talk to someone” first?
Find helpFinds the doctors screen and the emergency icon, with no hintCan they find help on their own?
Calm comfortChanges the tone to MinimalCan they find the setting? Does the tone feel right?

The Critical and emergency paths are not part of these sessions. They are tested separately, with a mental health professional present, before any launch.

Set the bar first. I would write down the pass mark for each task before the first session, so the result cannot be read to fit the design.

03  Accessibility

Colour is never the only signal.

Each stress state has a word on screen, not only a colour. Green, yellow and red must still read for someone who cannot tell them apart, so the label carries the meaning.

Contrast

Check every text and background pair against WCAG AA: 4.5 to 1 for normal text, 3 to 1 for large text. Check each screen, not only the colour tokens.

Text size

Body text is 14 px on a 20 px line, which is small. Test at the largest system font size, and make sure the cards still reflow without hiding an action.

Touch size

Tappable parts are at least 48 dp, as Episode 05 states. Check that the emergency icon is not crowded by the bottom bar.

Screen readers

Give the orb a spoken state name and the main button a clear action name, so the next step can be heard, not only seen.

Reduced motion

The orb’s motion carries the stress state. Offer a version that keeps the state word and the colour cue, with less movement.

04  Sensing and learning

Check the signal, then rank the actions.

The ring measures the body’s stress response, not feelings. The app must not rank actions for a person until the sensing has been checked and there is enough of that person’s own data.

Validate the sensing

Compare the ring’s readings with a reference measurement, with a specialist, in everyday conditions. Until then, the prototype’s heart rate stays simulated and says so.

Learning loop, later

After each session, the before and after heart rate would be stored, with consent, to learn which action calms this person best. Until there is enough data for one person, the app shows its default order.

05  Ethics and safety

Well-being support, never a diagnosis.

  • Not a medical device. The app supports wellbeing. It does not diagnose, and the wording says so.
  • Labelled AI. The companion is shown as a supportive companion, not a therapist, and hands off to a person when distress is high.
  • Opt-in sharing. Counselor and doctor sharing is chosen item by item, and can be revoked at any time.
  • General wellness advice. Sleep and food guidance is not medical or dietary advice.

06  Before any real launch

Five gates, in order.

A gate must pass before the next step starts. If a gate fails, the design changes before anyone uses it with real stress.

1. Usability5 to 8 people who feel stressed regularlyMost can follow the suggested action without help, against the pass mark set in advance
2. AccessibilityAn accessibility testerContrast, text size and screen reader checks pass on every screen
3. SensingA specialist, with real sensor dataThe readings match a reference, and their limits are stated in the app

07  What I learned

The hardest part was restraint.

Every feature I added to Home made it less calm. So the rule became: if it isn’t needed in the moment, it lives one tap away. Sleep, movement and food guidance, and the full meal plan, all sit in Insights for that reason.

The series ends here, but the design is not finished. The next thing I would make is not a screen. It is a test with five to eight people who feel stressed, and a record of what they did.
